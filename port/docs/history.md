# Publishing history

Why a 1999 PC RPG needed a publisher hunt, and when. Sources at the bottom; anything not
sourced is marked as unverified.

## Timeline

| When | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1994 | Team forms at **Viacom New Media** (Chicago); ships *MTV's Beavis and Butt-Head in Virtual Stupidity* | Valkyrie history |
| 1996-97 | *Septerra Core* in development at VNM; a **vertical slice demo** is built from existing VNM backgrounds and characters and used to shop the game to publishers | community record (unverified) |
| summer 1997 | Viacom announces it is **closing the Chicago studio**. The team forms **Valkyrie Studios**, acquires the rights to the game, and goes looking for a publisher | Valkyrie history |
| Dec 1998 | **Monolith Productions acquires North American publishing rights** | IGN |
| 1999 | Released on Windows. TopWare Interactive handles European retail, Monolith North America | PCGamingWiki, Wikipedia |

The window that matters for this project: between summer 1997 and December 1998 the game had
**no publisher signed**. That is when playtest feedback was being used in pitches.

## Unverified claims

These circulate in fan discussion and video descriptions, and are recorded here rather than
asserted:

- a **$1.2 million** deal with **TopWare Interactive** in 1997;
- an offer from **Electronic Arts** that would have rebranded the game under the **Ultima**
  franchise, declined to keep creative control.

TopWare's involvement as European publisher is documented; the figure and the EA offer are not.
